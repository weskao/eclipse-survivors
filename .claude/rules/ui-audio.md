# Mandatory UI sound and music review

When creating, modifying or removing ANY player-facing UI, interaction, dialog, button, menu, selector, HUD control, reward, onboarding step or gameplay flow, audio feedback is part of the UI/UX acceptance criteria. Cue taxonomy and coverage checklist: [`docs/es2/ui-audio-cue-matrix.md`](../../docs/es2/ui-audio-cue-matrix.md).

- Decide whether the action warrants sound, silence or a music transition. Reuse existing cues/tracks first (`src/game/audio/sfx.ts`, `src/game/audio/engine.ts`); add a distinct cue or locally owned audio asset only when a new event genuinely needs a recognizably different sound. Do not generate a new sound for every button.
- Standard buttons get one restrained `ui` cue; desktop mouse-entry hover may play `hover`. Use `data-ui-sfx="select"` for a meaningful local selection, `"scene"` for major navigation, `"managed"` when the validated action already plays its own semantic sound, `"silent"` when feedback would be intrusive. The central delegated click handler also covers keyboard and touch; **never manually add the generic cue in an individual component**.
- Gameplay transactions play their unique sound **only after success** (`start`, `pause`/`resume`, `level`, `upgrade`, `victory`/`defeat`, …). An invalid/denied action may use the quiet `deny` cue. Never layer a generic click over those cues or replay effects when the action is rejected.
- **Hero active skills (mandatory):** every current or future playable hero with an active skill must have its own recognizably distinct, dark-fantasy-appropriate cast SFX, registered in the shared audio catalog and mapped in its hero definition. Trigger exactly once at successful gameplay activation, from the same path for desktop Q and mobile touch; never on rejected casts, cooldown, pause, key repeat or per-frame effects. Keep the HUD action `data-ui-sfx="managed"`; do not stack a click, ordinary attack or dash cue as the cast sound. Use a bounded, finite one-shot synchronized to the cast animation, with no clipping, looping, new AudioContext or uncapped per-target sounds. Once a hero cast starts, its finite one-shot or fallback notes must finish through all in-run overlays (level-up, treasure, pause, settings, events, results); never stop or replay it because a gameplay screen changes. Cancel outstanding cast audio when returning Home, and continue honoring explicit mute/zero SFX volume, OS interruption and teardown. Preserve SFX mute/volume, interruption behavior and ordinary attack sounds; ensure cast feedback survives dense combat using the existing essential-cue policy. For hero actives, prefer original pre-rendered cinematic samples (impact/transient, role-specific body, spatial tail; not simple oscillator beeps or loudness alone). Store assets in `public/audio/heroes/`, preload/decode once per shared context, play one non-looping buffer on success with immediate bounded synth fallback if unavailable. Do not queue a delayed replay or mix the fallback with the sample. Preserve headroom (peak ≤ 0.85 full scale for the authored file), short tails and mobile memory bounds. Keep reproducible original source and provenance, and verify actual file bytes, runtime cue mapping, successful/rejected casts and mute; audition with combat music on desktop and mobile before declaring audio QA complete.
- **All combat skills and BOSS attacks:** use the same semantic event ownership principle for ordinary attacks, passives, active skills, evolutions, summons and enemy attacks. Prefer original layered, material-specific sound design aligned with real anticipation, release and meaningful contact; no generic beep as final art, no duplicate legacy + content cues, no per-frame audio triggers. Follow the attack-level gate in [boss-music-production.md](boss-music-production.md) for BOSSes and the combat visual fidelity skill for hero identity. Keep the existing one-context SFX/music ownership, voice caps and independent volume controls.
- No continuous beeping while dragging sliders, scrolling, moving a joystick, hovering descendants, holding a key, or per enemy in a crowd. Respect cooldown and polyphony caps; gameplay readability beats interface noise.
- Reuse **one shared audio owner** with the existing 0–100 independent music/SFX volumes, mute preferences, mobile trusted-gesture unlock, visibility interruption and teardown. Never create an AudioContext per component or layer legacy and ES2 scores on one scene. Music switches only at deliberate screen/game phase transitions, never autoplaying before a user gesture.
- Test mouse, touch, Enter/Space keyboard activation, `role=listbox` options, enabled/disabled states, settings, menu, modal, overlay, home/return, purchases, success/failure and mute, on desktop and mobile. Document each meaningful UI change's chosen cue (or why it stays silent); TDD asserts one action produces **at most one intended UI sound**.

- **Hero incoming damage:** ordinary, heavy, confirmed critical and extreme hits use one original material-layered finite PCM sample each, cached per existing AudioContext with immediate synth fallback only if rendering/playback fails. Keep the peak at or below 0.85 full scale, no looping/heartbeat, no double cue, no gameplay RNG for audio. Major-hit feedback is a brief directional amber impact, never the persistent red low-HP vignette. Respect SFX volume/mute, camera shake and accessibility preferences. Keep incoming critical rates/multipliers and post-mitigation loss thresholds in `src/game/balance/combat.json`.

- Hurt Foley revision gate: retain broadband transient, differentiated body/sub resonance, independent armour contact, damped early reflections, DC rejection and bounded fade. Test 44.1/48 kHz finite deterministic output, peak ≤ 0.85, RMS/crest factor and meaningful 120–220 ms tail energy; retain the listening gate, because waveform metrics alone cannot establish perceived production quality. Do not just boost the gain of a previous cue.

- **Terminal soundtrack:** enter the result screen and release the adaptive battle score before selecting the authored victory or defeat music. The host starts the first scheduling pass on settlement, independent of rendering and independent of result SFX; only the compatibility adapter may score results when the host lacks result support. Preserve the sampled victory anthem and defeat lament (`scripts/generate-victory-theme.mjs`, `scripts/generate-defeat-theme.mjs`), each with its finite synth fallback, independent mute/volume and menu return. Result music must be audible on phone/laptop speakers: keep most energy above 200 Hz (the old pure-sine 27–196 Hz defeat synth was effectively silent), asserted by the generator test. Guard both outcomes through real engine settlement with SFX muted and no rendered frame.

- If a run settles while Web Audio is suspended, retain at most the current unheard terminal SFX; clear it on leaving results, explicit SFX mute or teardown. Resume and schedule the original terminal BGM and SFX on successful context recovery, without a render-frame dependency or duplicate fanfare. For a previously running context unexpectedly suspended in the foreground, allow one result-scoped resume attempt; never create/unlock a fresh context automatically or override explicit visibility interruption. A trusted pointer/keyboard gesture remains the fallback. Exercise actual Boss Rush defeat, both buses, mute and stale-cue cancellation.

- **DEV result audio diagnostics:** append `?audioDiagnostics=1` to the development URL and filter DevTools Console for `[Audio][Result]`. Logs show outcome, actual Web Audio scheduling, context/resume state, independent mute/volume and post-gain music/SFX bus Peak and maximum window RMS (linear full-scale units). Sample every 50 ms for the first 12 seconds of results, report at most once per second plus the initial snapshot; stop and detach on leaving results or disposal. Null means no measurement, not silence. Bus measurements include residual battle/UI sounds and cannot establish physical speaker output. Keep disabled in production, never change routing, volume, assets or unlock policies for diagnostics.

## Continuous soundtrack and mobile performance

- **One soundtrack session per actual scene, independent of UI feedback.** Hero/mode selection, tabs, dialogs, HUD/joystick/dash/active-skill input, attacks, world-event choices and reward interactions must not pause, seek, restart, replace, reset the musical clock or change the BGM gain merely to play SFX. Treat `play` and `level` as one soundtrack session: opening, closing, skipping, claiming, declining or rerolling level/treasure/evolution choices preserve scene, mix, clocks and source. New-run initialization, genuine encounter/phase changes and terminal outcomes own music transitions. Preserve the existing deliberate pause/resume (including settings that pause gameplay), explicit mute/zero volume, Home, visibility/pagehide and OS interruption semantics.
- **Independent playback and idempotent recovery:** HTML media BGM can be healthy while Web Audio is suspended. In every mode, never force a media pause/play solely because AudioContext is `suspended`, newly created or awaiting `resume()`. Only an explicit lifecycle interruption or a previously running context reporting actual `interrupted` justifies the forced recovery path, at most once per pending resume. A genuinely paused active media element may still be started normally. Do not stop or recreate a live sampled Boss/warning/result source solely because the shared context is temporarily not running; let the same audio clock resume and apply genuine pending scene changes after recovery. Preserve intentional stop/mute and closed-context teardown. Use mouse pointerdown, touch/pen pointerup and completed control clicks for trusted recovery without duplicate cues.
- **Keep audio off the critical interaction/render path:** reuse bounded, sample-rate/context-aware decoded or pre-rendered buffers; for new or revised frequent UI/combat Foley, avoid synchronous PCM synthesis, decoding or large allocations inside gesture, render-frame or combat callbacks. Prepare outside time-critical interaction, or use an immediate bounded fallback without delayed duplicate playback. Maintain existing polyphony/cooldown caps and independent gains. Do not add a per-component context, per-frame fetch, unbounded cache or competing music loop. For procedural music, account for main-thread scheduling stalls and retain bounded catch-up; never hide regressions by turning off SFX, lowering visual quality or indiscriminately increasing scheduling/voice limits. Measure before changing audio/render budgets.
- **Explicit audio unlock button:** defer recovery on the button to its completed click for mouse, touch, pen, Enter and Space. The unlocked state removes the button, so earlier recovery can retarget or suppress the native click.
- **Regression gate:** assert track identity, play/pause count, position, source start/stop count, scene and music gain before/after repeated normal gestures, delayed/rejected resume, ordinary combat and Boss phase reward pickers, and recovery from genuine interruption. Test explicit mute/zero, pause/resume, result and Home separately. Use real engine transitions and cover the frames before the next intensity update. For changes affecting audio or workload, follow the physical-device and performance evidence procedure in [es-browser-qa](../skills/es-browser-qa/SKILL.md); state explicitly when device audio or profiling remains unverified.
