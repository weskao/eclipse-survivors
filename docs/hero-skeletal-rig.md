# Reusable humanoid rig (PixiJS v8, DEV candidate)
The runtime accepts immutable per-hero width, height, source weapon angle, canonical weapon hand, normalized left/right hip-knee-foot chains, and ten influence regions. The region order is weapon elbow, support hand, support elbow, torso, head, left knee, left foot, right knee, right foot, cape. Main grip is supplied by the art layer. No fixed Knight coordinates remain in the shared motion functions. The existing arm IK is selected by the base job; specializations will need their own arm solver when their equipment differs.

The optional directional layer set must contain E, SE, S, SW, W, NW, N, NE in independent body/weapon textures and per-view profile, grip and source blade angle. The validator rejects incomplete, duplicate or hand-swapped sets. Stationary retains the previous facing; attacks select aim while movement remains independent. The renderer precomputes all admitted meshes and samples the actual triangle coordinates for equipment sockets. Do not supply unreviewed cutouts as approved art. No automatic mirror for asymmetric equipment. Original art remains fallback.

For the current Knight profile the original single-view texture still supplies the live rig until actual directional body and weapon layers are imported, with hidden anatomy repaired. The new factory is shared, but only Knight is connected to real game state. Other heroes must supply and visually validate their own profiles and equipment, including live held-projectile behavior; a second synthetic profile is tested, not a production Ranger migration. Shader-based weapon tint respects reduceFlashing; world combat VFX remain existing Canvas effects. No second ticker. No production promotion until measured.

Art: native minimum 324x402 at Knight 108x134 logical px, transparent WebP via Polytool, same hand, uniform weapon scale, stable foot anchors, no texture stretching. Eight independently drawn full views are only source candidates; independent head, torso, upper/lower arms, hands, legs, cape and rigid sword still require authored separation and repair before final admission.

## Directional assets and VFX

Optional DEV asset import lives in experiments/pixijs-knight/assets/knight/. Manifest v1 lists 8 independent views with unique body/weapon filenames, grip, optional footAnchor, sourceAngle, weaponHand and per-view profile (legJoints/regions). The loader requires matching dimensions at least logical width × 3 and height × 3, no partial set, and explicitly admits QA_PENDING only for the opt-in candidate flag. Runtime uses the exact deformed mesh coordinate plus its body origin for the weapon pivot. It cannot infer hidden anatomy or prove pixels were genuinely authored at native resolution; review source art independently.

The local GPU accents include static slash, dash ring and special ring geometry plus weapon tint. Existing world swipes/projectiles/hit feedback remain unchanged. Geometry is built once, transforms/alpha update only on engine draws. No extra filter pass; reduced motion suppresses decorative effects and reduced flashing suppresses tint. Custom shader effects and full layer repaint are not complete; do not claim production approval.

## Locomotion and attack polish

Use distance-driven stance/swing rather than independently wiggling legs. The stance phase occupies 60% of a cycle; swing uses a smooth return and lift. Two-bone IK preserves both segment lengths and clamps targets to reach; this is approximate 2D contact, not world-space foot locking, and the full-body mesh still cannot reconstruct occluded armor. Lower-body gait continues during moving attacks. Real windup/hold may ease the blade into preparation, the existing contact tick is unchanged, and follow-through eases to neutral. No root motion, damage delay or movement balance change. Direction selection responds immediately to the travel octant while the bone motion blends. The actor framebuffer defaults to device density capped at 3; ?knightRigResolution=3 gives the previous fixed-density comparison. Source 3x artwork is never downscaled on disk. Browser QA now establishes a deterministic enemy-free locomotion scenario and uses actual combat for all eight attack directions. Mobile portrait/landscape candidate paths are exercised. Production remains unchanged pending visual and physical-device evidence.

## Anatomical contact and hinge constraints

References: [AAPM&R normal gait biomechanics](https://now.aapmr.org/biomechanics-normal-gait/), [Spine IK constraints](https://en.esotericsoftware.com/spine-ik-constraints), [Spine eight-direction east walk rig](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m-0wgqKquiQ), [Game Developer animation principles](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/the-12-principles-of-animation-in-video-games).

The previous 2D chain treated the ankle as the whole boot, lacked flexion bounds, and used a different sword arc from the arm. The candidate now has separate heel/toe points, stance target cancellation at full steady gait, heel roll/toe push-off and swing clearance, fixed authored bend pole, projected knee/elbow angle limits, and a shared attack curve for wrist target and blade angle. Real combat hit time is unchanged. All eight view anchors were adjusted against the existing 400x500 art grid; hidden limbs remain estimated. The ankle pivots and foot contact values are provisional, not mocap or anatomically verified 3D joint angles. Projected limits include the neutral authored angle to avoid rest-pose jumps. Reach clamping, turns, slowing and blended mesh weights can still cause residual slide; this is not a world-space foot lock. QA-only skeleton overlay: ?knightPixi=1&knightRigCandidates=1&knightPixiQA=1&knightRigBones=1. The body remains a single mesh; independently authored feet/limbs and occlusion repair are still needed for final visual approval. Four added pure regression tests, 8/8 locally verified. No production or other hero changes.

### Projected knee plane

A 2.5D sagittal-chain correction uses the eight authored view angles. In a side view the knee bends in the forward screen direction; in front/rear views its bend is principally into depth. Local 3D segment lengths remain fixed; only the on-screen segment length changes through foreshortening. The leg skin transform follows that projected segment end-to-end without changing its perpendicular width. Blend from the authored neutral bend plane to the facing plane as gait activity rises. This is an approximation of physical movement, not a 3D skeleton reconstructed from the illustration. Two added regression tests exercise all eight projected poles and attachment preservation (10/10 human-motion tests locally verified).

### Speed-dependent cadence and pelvis reach

Knight speed is 190 game px/s, while the old visual stride length was 134*0.39=52.26 px, implying approximately 7.27 footfalls/s at full speed. The new integrated gait advances from actual distance deltas with continuous phase, transitions from 60% walk support to 48% fast combat-run support, caps cadence at 2.1 cycles/s, and stops rapid stepping during dash. At the existing default speed it predicts 190/(134*0.90)*2 ≈ 3.15 footfalls/s. No gameplay speed or hit timing changed. Pelvis reach correction lowers the visual body (not gameplay position) up to 9% of height on support legs; screen-depth stride is foreshortened to reduce knee overextension in north/south views. These are animation tuning values, not clinical human motion-capture parameters. Actual contact can still slip when changing speed/direction or reaching limits. Three new cadence/reach regression tests (13/13 human-motion tests verified locally).

### Ankle/boot attachment correction

Actual directional manifest regression sampled 100 phases × 8 views × 3 travel/attack headings × 2 legs. Previous lower-body weighting could pull the ankle toward the other foot; an independent pitch could exceed the projected shin rotation limit. Lower leg owns the nearest ankle and boot, both transforms share the solved ankle pivot. The visual target excursion is reduced if projected shin rotation exceeds 40 degrees, with boot tilt capped at 18 degrees and relative ankle rotation capped at 30 degrees from authored rest. Pitch uses the selected art view rather than movement heading so strafing attacks do not flip the boot. These are conservative projected animation limits, not medical joint measurements. Gameplay position and speed are unchanged; shortened visual steps can still slip. Exact joint-sample regression is not a guarantee that the coarse full-body mesh has no visible distortion.
